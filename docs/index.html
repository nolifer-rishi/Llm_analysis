Evaluating Small Language Models as Educational Tutors

A fully local, multi-metric pipeline analyzing the performance of 9 small language models on complex reasoning and factual recall tasks.

5,400+ Questions Evaluated
5 Diverse Datasets
9 Local Models Evaluated

Abstract

The EduBench-Local project aims to evaluate the viability of deploying small, open-weight Language Models (such as Gemma, Llama 3, Qwen, and DeepSeek) on edge hardware (AMD Radeon GPUs via Vulkan) for educational tutoring. By avoiding cloud APIs, we ensure privacy and zero recurring inference costs.

This study evaluates over 5,400 queries across five standardized educational datasets spanning grade-school science to reading comprehension. To capture a holistic view of performance, we employ a multi-metric approach: ROUGE-L (lexical overlap), BERTScore (semantic similarity), and a novel LLM-as-a-Judge prompting paradigm.

Methodology

📚

Datasets

We sampled 100 questions from 6 datasets to cover various domains:

  • ARC (Grade Science)
  • OpenBookQA (Science Reasoning)
  • SciQ (Factual Science)
  • RACE (Reading Comprehension)
  • SQuAD (Extractive QA)
⚙️

Inference

All inference was executed entirely locally:

  • Models: 9 distinct Local LLMs (1.5B to 120B params)
  • Engine: Ollama with Vulkan Backend
  • Hardware: AMD Radeon RX 6500M (4GB VRAM)
  • Avg Latency: Variable per model architecture
📊

Metrics

A multi-faceted evaluation pipeline:

  • ROUGE-L: Exact text sequence overlap.
  • BERTScore: Deep semantic embedding similarity (F1).
  • LLM-Judge: Mistral 7B evaluating its own answers on a 1-5 scale for factual correctness.

Key Insights

1. Multi-Metric Divergence

We discovered a Very Strong correlation (ρ=0.81) between ROUGE-L and BERTScore, but a Weak correlation (ρ~0.3) between the LLM-Judge and the text-overlap metrics. This validates our methodology: the LLM-Judge successfully evaluates factual correctness rather than punishing the model for merely phrasing the answer differently than the reference text.

2. Edge Viability

Despite being quantized and running on limited hardware (4GB VRAM), several models (such as Gemma 2 2B and Qwen 2.5) achieved exceptional Mean Judge Scores above 4.5/5. This strongly supports the viability of local LLMs for educational applications where privacy and cost are primary concerns.
